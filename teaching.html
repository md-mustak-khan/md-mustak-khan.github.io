🎓 Teaching Philosophy

My Approach to Education

How I teach and what students can expect

I believe in hands-on, problem-solving approaches to bioinformatics education. My courses bridge the gap between theoretical concepts and real research workflows, ensuring students leave with practical skills they can apply immediately in their own projects.

Science education should be accessible, rigorous, and connected to real-world challenges. Every module I design is built around a concrete research problem — not abstract theory — so students understand why each tool or technique matters before learning how to use it.

My goal is to train the next generation of computational biologists who can think critically, work independently, and contribute meaningfully to global health research.

In Silico Innovation: Computer-Aided Drug Design and Network Pharmacology

Role: Instructor Platform: BioPC — Bioinformatics Lab of Research & Training

A specialized course focused on computational approaches to modern drug discovery. The course integrates bioinformatics, computer-aided drug design (CADD), and network pharmacology to provide rigorous theoretical and practical training in systems-level drug discovery. Students work with real-world datasets and industry-standard tools throughout every module.

Course Modules

Intro to CADD Ligand-Based Drug Design Structure-Based Drug Design Network Pharmacology Target Identification ADMET Prediction Molecular Docking Virtual Screening Visualization Tools

Course Outcomes

  • Understand core concepts of computer-aided drug design and network pharmacology
  • Apply bioinformatics tools including AutoDock Vina, SwissADME, and Cytoscape for target analysis and molecular interpretation
  • Develop a stronger foundation in translational bioinformatics and interdisciplinary research

Who Should Attend

Graduate Students in Biochemistry / Pharmacology Researchers in Computational Drug Discovery Early-Career Bioinformaticians
