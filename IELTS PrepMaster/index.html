Academic vs General Training

আপনার লক্ষ্য অনুযায়ী সঠিক টেস্ট নির্বাচন করুন:Choose the right test based on your objective:

  • Academic (AC): বিশ্ববিদ্যালয় ভর্তি (অনার্স/মাস্টার্স) এবং প্রফেশনাল লাইসেন্সিং (ডাক্তার, নার্স, ইঞ্জিনিয়ার)-এর জন্য প্রযোজ্য।Required for university admissions (Undergrad/Masters) and professional licensing (Doctors, Nurses, Engineers).
  • General Training (GT): ইমিগ্রেশন (কানাডা এক্সপ্রেস এন্ট্রি, অস্ট্রেলিয়া পিআর) এবং চাকরির অভিজ্ঞতার জন্য প্রযোজ্য।Designed for immigration (Canada Express Entry, Australia PR) and work experience programs.
  • কমন মডিউল:Shared Modules: Listening এবং Speaking পরীক্ষা AC ও GT উভয়ের জন্যই ১০০% এক। শুধু Reading এবং Writing Task 1 আলাদা।Listening and Speaking are 100% identical for both AC & GT. Only Reading and Writing Task 1 differ.
প্রো-টিপ:Pro Tip: একাডেমিক রিডিংয়ে ভোকাবুলারি তুলনামূলক জটিল হওয়ায় জিটি-র চেয়ে কিছুটা কম সঠিক উত্তরেও ভালো ব্যান্ড পাওয়া যায়।Academic Reading vocabulary is more complex, so fewer raw correct answers are needed compared to GT to achieve the same band score.

পরীক্ষার সময় ও স্ট্রাকচারTest Duration & Structure

মোট পরীক্ষার সময়: আনুমানিক ২ ঘণ্টা ৪৫ মিনিট (মাঝে কোনো বিরতি নেই):Total test duration: Approximately 2 hours 45 minutes (no breaks between tests):

  • Listening (৩০ মিনিট):Listening (30 mins): ৪টি সেকশন, ৪০টি প্রশ্ন। অডিও মাত্র একবার বাজবে। CD-IELTS এ শেষে উত্তর চেক করার জন্য ২ মিনিট সময় পাওয়া যায়।4 sections, 40 questions. Audio played once only. CD-IELTS allows 2 minutes at the end to review answers.

ব্যান্ড ৯ বনাম ব্যান্ড ৬ এসে বিশ্লেষণ ও পরীক্ষকের মূল্যায়ন Band 9 vs Band 6 Comparative Essay Analysis & Examiner Scoring

Side-by-Side Rubric
"Some people believe that universities should focus on providing knowledge and skills related to students' future careers, while others think that the true function of a university is to provide access to knowledge for its own sake, regardless of practical utility. Discuss both views and give your opinion."
Official Band 9.0 Model Essay
While a pragmatic perspective contends that higher education must primarily serve as an incubator for vocational competencies, an alternative philosophical school champions tertiary learning as an intrinsic intellectual pursuit. Although acquiring employable expertise is indispensable in an increasingly competitive global economy, I would argue that universities must cultivate broader intellectual curiosity while fostering specialized workplace readiness. Proponents of career-oriented curricula highlight the immediate economic exigencies modern graduates confront. As professional landscapes become ever more automated and specialized, institutions of higher learning are obligated to equip undergraduates with targeted technical prowess, data literacy, and industry-aligned skills. For instance, medical faculties and software engineering departments naturally prioritize empirical competencies over abstract philosophy. Consequently, tertiary education is viewed as a substantial personal investment whose ROI is validated through gainful employment. Conversely, the classical humanist tradition emphasizes that unconstrained scholastic inquiry forms the cornerstone of civil society. Beyond preparing individuals for specific vocations, academic exploration across humanities and theoretical sciences cultivates critical thinking, analytical discernment, and ethical judgment. A university unconstrained by corporate utility can incubate ground-breaking innovations; historically, fundamental discoveries in theoretical physics or pure mathematics occurred without immediate commercial objectives. In conclusion, while career preparation represents a vital utilitarian function of tertiary institutions, confining higher education solely to vocational training risks impoverishing society’s intellectual vitality. The optimal academic paradigm seamlessly harmonizes rigorous practical training with holistic, curiosity-driven scholarship.

Examiner Commentary (Band 9.0):

  • Task Response (9.0): Fully develops all parts of prompt with a sustained, nuanced, and mature perspective throughout.
  • Coherence & Cohesion (9.0): Seamless logical progression with sophisticated referencing without redundant mechanical linkers.
  • Lexical Resource (9.0): Rare academic collocations used effortlessly (pragmatic perspective, vocational competencies, unconstrained scholastic inquiry, utilitarian function).
  • Grammar & Accuracy (9.0): Flawless command of complex multi-clause structures, inversion, and subjunctive syntax.
Typical Band 6.0 Candidate Essay
Nowadays, people have different opinions about universities. Some people think universities should teach jobs skills, but other people believe they should just give knowledge to students. In this essay, I will discuss both sides and give my personal view. On the one hand, getting a job after graduation is very important for every student. Nowadays, life is very expensive and tuition fees are high, so students want to get good salaries. If universities only teach theory, students cannot find jobs easily in modern companies. For example, my brother studied engineering and he learned practical things, so he got a good job immediately. Therefore, universities should focus on jobs. On the other hand, knowledge is also good for everyone. If people only study for money, they will not understand history, art or science. Also, knowledge makes people open-minded and educated citizens. For example, reading philosophy helps people think better. If nobody studies pure science, we cannot make new discoveries in the future. In conclusion, both sides have good points. In my opinion, universities should teach skills for future careers because students need money to live, but general knowledge should also be included a little bit.

Examiner Commentary (Band 6.0):

  • Task Response (6.0): Addresses all parts of task, but arguments are somewhat simplistic and personal (anecdotal evidence: "my brother studied").
  • Coherence & Cohesion (6.0): Uses mechanical and repetitive cohesive devices (Nowadays, On the one hand, On the other hand, In conclusion).
  • Lexical Resource (6.0): Adequate vocabulary with repetitive words (good, job, practical things, money to live); lacks band 7+ academic collocations.
  • Grammar & Accuracy (6.0): Simple sentences with occasional basic punctuation and run-on phrasing; lacks sophisticated clause subordination.
